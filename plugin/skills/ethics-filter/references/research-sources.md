# Research Sources for Ethics Filter Modules

Each module in the ethics filter is grounded in established human ethics frameworks. This reference captures the source material for future refinement and auditability.

## Module Sources

### Module 1: Environmental Stewardship
- **B Corp Environment criteria** (B Lab) — impact areas for climate, land, water, biodiversity
- **ESG frameworks** (Global standards) — Environmental metrics and disclosures
- **Triple Bottom Line** (Elkington, 1994) — People, Planet, Profit
- **IFOAM Ecology principle** — "Organic agriculture should be based on living ecological systems and cycles"
- **Circular economy principles** (Ellen MacArthur Foundation) — eliminate waste, circulate materials, regenerate nature

### Module 2: Fairness & Stakeholder Impact
- **B Corp Workers + Community criteria** (B Lab) — fair wages, worker welfare, community impact
- **IFOAM Fairness principle** — equity, respect, justice for all
- **Stakeholder Theory** (Freeman, 1984) — businesses must serve all stakeholders, not just shareholders
- **Conscious Capitalism** (Mackey & Sisodia, 2007) — stakeholder orientation as core principle
- **Rawls' Justice as Fairness** (1971) — veil of ignorance, difference principle, fair equality of opportunity

### Module 3: Transparency & Accountability
- **B Corp Governance criteria** (B Lab) — mission lock, stakeholder governance, transparency
- **F-A-T-H-E-R Leadership** (Moore) — Fairness, Accountability, Trust, Honesty, Equality, Respect
- **Markkula Publicity Test** — "Would I want my choice published in the newspaper?"
- **Transparency International standards** — anti-corruption, disclosure, accountability

### Module 4: Conscious Leadership & Values Alignment
- **15 Commitments of Conscious Leadership** (Dethmer, Chapman, Klemp) — above/below the line framework
- **Conscious Capitalism** (Mackey & Sisodia, 2007) — purpose-driven leadership, stakeholder orientation
- **Values-Based Leadership** (Kraemer) — self-reflection, balance, true self-confidence, genuine humility
- **F-A-T-H-E-R Framework** — Fairness, Accountability, Trust, Honesty, Equality, Respect

### Module 5: Ethical Decision Framework (Meta-Module)
- **Markkula Center 5-Step Framework** (Santa Clara University) — Recognize Issue, Get Facts, Evaluate Alternatives, Make Decision, Act & Reflect
- **5 Ethical Lenses** (Markkula) — Utilitarian, Rights, Fairness, Common Good, Virtue
- **7-Step Method** (Michael Davis, Illinois Tech) — State Problem, Gather Facts, Identify Stakeholders, List Options, Test Options, Make Decision, Act
- **IDEA Framework** (Trillium Health Partners) — I = Identify, D = Determine, E = Evaluate, A = Act
- **IFOAM Care principle** — "managed in a precautionary and responsible manner"

### Module 6: Compliance & Certification Guard
- **B Corp certification standards** (B Lab) — recertification every 3 years
- **Fair Trade certification** — supply chain equity, fair prices, decent working conditions
- **Organic certification general** — IFOAM family of standards, national organic programs
- **ISO standards** — 14001 (environmental), 26000 (social responsibility)
- **SA8000** — social accountability, workers' rights, no forced labour

## Philosophical Grounding

The filter does not privilege any single ethical tradition. It surfaces tensions between:
- **Deontological ethics** (Rights lens) — duties and rules
- **Consequentialist ethics** (Utilitarian lens) — outcomes and consequences
- **Virtue ethics** (Virtue lens) — character and integrity
- **Justice ethics** (Fairness lens) — equitable distribution
- **Communitarian ethics** (Common Good lens) — community and belonging

The conflict resolution step (Step 4) is where these tensions are surfaced for human judgment — the filter does not resolve them automatically.
