# Ethics Frameworks — Research Reference

This file documents the real-world ethics frameworks that inform the ethics-filter modules. 
Use this when extending modules, adding new ones, or justifying design decisions.

## B Corp Impact Assessment (B Lab, 2006-present)

**Source:** bcorporation.net / B Lab

Five impact areas that underpin B Corp certification:
- **Governance** — Mission, ethics, transparency, stakeholder accountability
- **Workers** — Financial security, health & safety, career development, engagement
- **Community** — Local engagement, diversity, equity, supply chain impact
- **Environment** — Carbon, water, waste, land use, circularity
- **Customers** — Product impact, quality assurance, stewardship

Scoring: 80+ points required for certification (250 weighted questions).

Used by: ethics-filter modules on Fairness, Transparency, Compliance, Environmental.

## Markkula Center Framework (Santa Clara University)

**Source:** scu.edu/ethics

**5-Step Decision Process:**
1. Recognize the Ethical Issue
2. Get the Facts
3. Evaluate Alternative Actions
4. Make a Decision and Test It
5. Act and Reflect on the Outcome

**5 Ethical Lenses:**
- **Utilitarian** — Greatest good for greatest number
- **Rights** — Respect for fundamental rights and dignity
- **Fairness/Justice** — Equitable distribution of benefits and burdens
- **Common Good** — Contribution to community and shared values
- **Virtue** — Character, integrity, and moral ideals

Used by: ethics-filter Module 5 (Ethical Framework).

## IFOAM Principles of Organic Agriculture (IFOAM, 1972)

**Source:** ifoam.bio

Four interconnected ethical principles:
- **Health** — Sustain and enhance soil, plant, animal, human, planetary health
- **Ecology** — Work with living ecological systems and cycles
- **Fairness** — Ensure fairness regarding common environment and life opportunities
- **Care** — Manage in precautionary and responsible manner for future generations

Used by: ethics-filter modules (philosophical foundation, not domain-specific).

## Conscious Capitalism / Conscious Leadership

**Sources:** 
- Mackey & Sisodia, "Conscious Capitalism" (2007)
- Dethmer, Chapman & Klemp, "The 15 Commitments of Conscious Leadership"
- consciouscapitalism.org

**Four Tenets of Conscious Capitalism:**
1. Higher Purpose (beyond profit)
2. Stakeholder Orientation (all stakeholders, not just shareholders)
3. Conscious Leadership (above-the-line leadership)
4. Conscious Culture (trust, authenticity, caring)

**Above/Below the Line Framework:**
- Above: Open, curious, committed, ownership, learning
- Below: Closed, defensive, right, blame, victim

**15 Commitments include:** Radical responsibility, curiosity, candor, integrity, appreciation, acknowledgment.

Used by: ethics-filter Module 4 (Conscious Leadership).

## Holistic Management (Savory Institute, 1980s)

**Source:** savory.global

A whole-system decision-making framework:
- Define your holistic goal (quality of life + future resource base)
- Test decisions against the goal with 7 context checks
- Plan, monitor, and adapt

Used by: ethics-filter systems-thinking approach, tension resolution in pipeline.

## F-A-T-H-E-R Ethical Leadership

**Source:** Barry D. Moore / greatworklife.com

Six core values:
- **F**airness
- **A**ccountability
- **T**rust
- **H**onesty
- **E**quality
- **R**espect

Used by: ethics-filter Module 3 (Transparency & Accountability) and Module 2 (Fairness).

## ESG / Triple Bottom Line

**Sources:** Global standards (GRI, SASB, TCFD)

Three pillars:
- **People** (Social) — Labour, human rights, community, diversity
- **Planet** (Environmental) — Climate, emissions, waste, water, biodiversity
- **Profit** (Economic) — Sustainable economic value, governance, ethics

Used by: ethics-filter Module 1 (Environmental) and Module 6 (Compliance).

## Australian Organic Certification Standards

**Sources:** ACO (Australian Certified Organic), NASAA, ACOS

Key requirements:
- No synthetic pesticides, herbicides, or fertilisers
- No GMOs
- Annual on-site audits
- Full supply chain traceability
- Compliance with National Standard

Used by: ethics-filter Module 6 (Compliance) certification reference.

## Australian National Standard for Organic Produce

**Sources:** Australian Government Department of Agriculture

The baseline standard for organic exports. Used by ACO, NASAA, SXC as the foundation for organic certification in Australia.

## Regenerative Agriculture Principles

**Sources:** Terra Genesis International, NRDC, Rodale Institute, Savory

Four consistent principles:
1. Enhance and improve soil health
2. Optimise resource management
3. Alleviate climate change
4. Improve water quality and availability

Plus community equity and continuous progress.

Used by: ethics-filter Module 1 (Environmental) philosophical foundation.
